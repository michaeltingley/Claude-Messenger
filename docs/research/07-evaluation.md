## 1. Memory / long-horizon benchmarks — what exists, what it misses, and why vendor numbers can't be trusted

### 1.1 The benchmarks

| Benchmark | What it measures | Scale | Key gap for a real personal corpus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LongMemEval** ([arXiv:2410.10813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813), [site](https://xiaowu0162.github.io/long-mem-eval/), [code](https://github.com/xiaowu0162/LongMemEval)) | 5 abilities: information extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates, **abstention**; 500 curated questions in scalable chat histories (115K → 1.5M tokens). Frames memory as indexing → retrieval → reading. Found commercial assistants drop ~30% accuracy over sustained interactions. | 115K (S) / ~500 sessions (M) | Synthetic user-assistant chat, not multi-channel comms; at 115K tokens, full-context baselines are competitive, so it partly measures context organization, not memory ([critique](https://essays.bloo-mind.ai/posts/2026-05-20-mem-eval/)) |
| **LoCoMo** ([overview](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/locomo-benchmark)) | QA + event summarization + timeline over multi-session synthetic dialogues (~300 turns, ~9K tokens/conv, up to 35 sessions) | ~1,540 Qs / small per-conv context | LLM-generated, keyword-rich language that flatters exact-match retrieval; consistent character naming makes NER unusually effective; 97% of oracle traces are single-hop; documented gold-answer errors ([LTM-for-LCQA paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.23730), [LoCoMo-Plus](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10715v1)) |
| **MemBench** ([ACL 2025 Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.989/), [arXiv:2506.21605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21605)) | Factual + **reflective** memory, participation vs. observation scenarios; scores effectiveness, efficiency, capacity; 1K–100K token contexts | mid-size | Still scripted scenarios; doesn't model a private heterogeneous corpus |
| **GoodAI LTM Benchmark** ([GitHub](https://github.com/GoodAI/goodai-ltm-benchmark), [blog](https://www.goodai.com/introducing-goodai-ltm-benchmark/)) | Dynamic, *interleaved* tasks inside one continuously growing conversation — memory upkeep, integration over time, distraction resistance | very long single conversation | Closest in spirit to "living with an agent," but tasks are synthetic scripted probes |
| **MSC (Multi-Session Chat)** ([Xu et al., ACL 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.356/), [arXiv:2107.07567](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07567)) | Human-human 5-session chats with persona summaries; showed standard models fail at long-term continuity, retrieval+summarization wins | ~1K tokens/session | Persona consistency only; tiny by 2026 standards; foundational but obsolete as a target |
| **BEAM (2026)** ([Mem0 benchmark roundup](https://mem0.ai/blog/ai-memory-benchmarks-in-2026)) | 10 capabilities incl. fact tracking, entity updates, contradiction resolution, temporal ordering, multi-hop; up to 10M tokens, 100 convs, 2,000 probes; "no current memory architecture saturates it" | 1M/10M tracks | New; still synthetic conversation, still QA-probe format |
| **PerLTQA** ([arXiv:2402.16288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16288), [ACL SIGHAN 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.sighan-1.18/)) | Semantic + episodic personal memory (profiles, relationships, events, dialogues); 8,593 Qs over 30 synthetic characters; splits the task into memory classification / retrieval / synthesis | 30 personas | Synthetic characters, not a real person's corpus; but its **task decomposition** (classify → retrieve → synthesize) is a useful eval decomposition for your architecture |

Newer 2026 entrants worth tracking: **LongMemEval-V2** ([arXiv:2605.12493](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.12493)), **LoCoMo-Plus** (beyond-factual "cognitive memory" with implicit constraints, [arXiv:2602.10715](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10715v1)), **KnowMe-Bench** (person-understanding for lifelong companions, [arXiv:2601.04745](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04745)), and streaming/on-policy benches (**StreamMemBench** [arXiv:2606.14571](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.14571), **EvoMemBench** [arXiv:2605.18421](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18421)).

### 1.2 How memory vendors actually evaluate, and the documented mess

- **Mem0** ([arXiv:2504.19413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19413), ECAI 2025) evaluates on LoCoMo with F1, BLEU-1, and an **LLM-as-a-Judge "J" score**, plus p95 latency (−91% vs full-context) and token cost (<7K tokens/retrieval vs 25K+ full-context) ([Mem0 research page](https://mem0.ai/research)). The accuracy-plus-cost framing is the right *shape*; the numbers are the contested part.
- **Zep vs Mem0 war**: Zep's rebuttal ["Is Mem0 Really SOTA?"](https://blog.getzep.com/lies-damn-lies-statistics-is-mem0-really-sota-in-agent-memory/) claimed 84% on LoCoMo; Mem0 documented an arithmetic error (adversarial Category 5 in numerator but not denominator, ~25pt inflation) and measured 58.44% ± 0.20 over 10 seeds ([GitHub issue on zep-papers](https://github.com/getzep/zep-papers/issues/5)); Zep counter-claimed 75.14% after fixing Mem0's misconfiguration of Zep. Nobody independent can reproduce anybody.
- **Letta's null result**: an agent just storing conversation history **in files and grepping** scored 74.0% on LoCoMo with GPT-4o-mini — above Mem0's best graph variant — leading Letta to argue LoCoMo-class benchmarks mostly measure context management, not memory architecture ([Letta blog](https://www.letta.com/blog/benchmarking-ai-agent-memory/)).
- **The "Benchmark Theatre" synthesis** ([The Hidden Layer, May 2026](https://essays.bloo-mind.ai/posts/2026-05-20-mem-eval/)) documents: 6.4% corrupted gold answers in LoCoMo (99/1,540, capping the max at ~93.6% — making several claimed scores mathematically impossible); the standard GPT-4o-mini judge accepting **62.81% of intentionally wrong-but-topical answers**; unreproducible vendor claims (EverMemOS 92.32% official vs 38.38% third-party); single-trial point estimates where the small categories need ~15pt gaps for significance; and on-policy benchmarks (MemoryBench, AMemGym, MEMTRACK) showing memory systems often **don't beat RAG baselines** and off-policy rankings disagree with on-policy rankings.

**Takeaways for you:** (a) public memory benchmarks are useful as *smoke tests* and for harness design patterns (LongMemEval's 5-ability taxonomy, abstention scoring, indexing/retrieval/reading decomposition), not as architecture-selection evidence; (b) **always run full-context and filesystem-grep baselines on your own data first** — a memory architecture must beat those by a wide margin to justify itself; (c) multi-seed runs with error bars, and a judge validated against wrong-but-topical distractors, are table stakes.

## 2. Personalization benchmarks & style-fidelity measurement

### 2.1 Personalization benchmarks

- **LaMP** ([arXiv:2304.11406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11406), [ACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.399/)): 7 tasks (3 classification, 4 generation) with per-user profiles — citation ID, headline generation, tweet paraphrasing, etc. The canonical protocol: retrieve from the user's history, generate, compare against the user's actual output. **LongLaMP** extends to long-form personalized generation ([arXiv:2407.11016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.11016)); **LaMP-QA** covers personalized long-form QA ([arXiv:2506.00137](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00137v1)). LaMP's key transferable idea: **the user's own historical artifacts are simultaneously the retrieval corpus and the gold labels.**
- **PersonaBench** ([arXiv:2502.20616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20616), [ACL 2025 Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.49.pdf)): synthetic private user data (conversation logs, chats, purchase records) + questions about biography/preferences/relationships; finds RAG pipelines **struggle to extract personal information from user documents**. Relevant negative result: retrieval over personal data is genuinely hard, not solved by default RAG.
- **PersonaMem / "Know Me, Respond to Me"** ([arXiv:2504.14225](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14225v2)): dynamic user profiling where facts *change over time* — the eval checks the model uses the *latest* state. Directly relevant to relationship modeling (people move, change jobs, change tone with you).
- 2026 wave: **MCP-Persona** (agents on simulated personal apps, [arXiv:2606.02470](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02470v1)), **BehaviorBench** (predicting real user decisions from behavioral traces, [arXiv:2606.02798](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.02798)), **ImplicitMemBench** (does the system *adapt behavior* without being asked, [arXiv:2604.08064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08064)) — the field is moving from "recall the fact" to "act on the person," which is your actual product claim.

### 2.2 Style fidelity: how to score "sounds like me"

The central paper is **"Evaluating Style-Personalized Text Generation: Challenges and Directions"** ([arXiv:2508.06374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06374)): builds a style-discrimination benchmark over 8 writing tasks and 3 settings (domain discrimination, authorship attribution, personalized-vs-non-personalized discrimination). Findings:
- **LLM judges do not capture author-specific style well** — a documented weakness, on top of the generic judge biases below.
- **An ensemble of n-gram matching + style embeddings + LLM judge beats any single evaluator by up to 12%.** No single "voice-match" metric is trustworthy alone.

Tooling for the automatic-judge stack:
- **Style embeddings**: **StyleDistance** — content-independent style embeddings trained on synthetic parallel pairs ([arXiv:2410.12757](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12757v2)); **LUAR** — widely used but *confounds style and content* ([discussion in 2508.06374](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.06374)); the **STEL** protocol tests whether a representation matches same-style texts independent of content.
- **Authorship verification (AV) as judge**: SOTA circa 2025-26 includes **SELMA** (LLM-embedding AV, training-free, per the [2025-26 AV literature](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.194/)), **InstructAV** (instruction-tuned AV with explanations, [arXiv:2407.12882](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.12882)), cross-genre benchmark **CROSSNEWS** ([AAAI 2025](https://cocoxu.github.io/publications/AAAI_2025_Cross_genre_Authorship.pdf)). Caveat that cuts both ways: a March 2026 study found LLM-prompted impersonation does **not** reliably fool AV methods ([arXiv:2603.29454](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.29454)) — good news, AV verifiers are a meaningful hurdle, so "does an AV model accept the draft as written-by-you" is a usable automatic voice-match metric.
- **"Theory-Grounded Evaluation Exposes the Authorship Gap in LLM Personalization"** ([arXiv:2604.26460](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.26460)): 2026 evidence that surface metrics overstate personalization quality — LLM outputs pass topical checks while failing authorship-theoretic ones. Supports using AV/stylometry, not just judges.

### 2.3 LLM-as-judge biases (so you can control them)

Documented, quantified biases: **position bias** (swapping order shifts pairwise accuracy >10%; studied across 15 judges / ~150K instances, [Justice or Prejudice / CALM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02736), [IJCNLP 2025 position-bias work](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.02219)); **verbosity bias** (judges preferred lengthened-but-not-more-informative answers >90% of the time, [Adaline summary](https://www.adaline.ai/blog/llm-as-a-judge-reliability-bias)); **self-preference/family bias** ([EMNLP 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.02592), [statistical method](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.06709), [arXiv:2410.21819](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21819)); plus the CALM framework's catalog of 12 judge biases. Mitigations that are now standard: randomize/swap pair order and average, pin judge model+temperature, use rubric-anchored pointwise scoring with reference answers, use a judge from a different model family than the generator, and validate the judge on a small human-labeled anchor set.

## 3. Retrieval eval on a private corpus — and prior art for your killer eval

### 3.1 Prior art: "the reply the user actually sent" as supervision

Your idea has real lineage, though nobody has published it in exactly your framing ("context-assembly eval for a personal agent"). The pieces:

1. **Next-response selection** (the oldest form): Ubuntu/Douban-corpus retrieval chatbots were evaluated by whether the **true response** ranks in the top-k among candidates (Recall@k), and matching models were *trained with weak supervision from true responses* ([Wu et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.02333), [dialogue eval survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.04071), [full-rank response retrieval](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.10558)). This is exactly "the sent reply is the label," applied to response ranking rather than context assembly.
2. **LaMP protocol**: user's actual historical outputs are gold labels for personalized generation; retrieval quality is measured *indirectly* by downstream generation gains ([LaMP](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.399/), [retrieval-augmentation optimization for LaMP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.05970)).
3. **Panza** ([arXiv:2407.10994](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10994), [GitHub IST-DASLab/PanzaMail](https://github.com/IST-DASLab/PanzaMail)) — the closest single piece of prior art for your whole product: a fully-local personal email assistant fine-tuned+RAG'd on the user's own sent mail via "data playback" (summarize each sent email into an instruction, regenerate, compare against **the email the user actually wrote** as the golden response, scored with BLEU/ROUGE). Counterfactual held-out-sent-mail eval, on real personal data, done in 2024.
4. **EnronSR** ([ICWSM 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/31448), [PDF](https://sel.sise.bgu.ac.il/assets/pubs/enron-sr-icwsm-2024.pdf)): benchmark of naturally occurring human replies + AI replies for the same Enron messages — a public reference implementation of "compare generated reply to the reply actually sent," including comparison against production Google Smart Reply.
5. **Gmail Smart Compose/Smart Reply** ([KDD 2019, arXiv:1906.00080](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00080)): the canonical industrial statement of the problem you'll face — they explicitly built **offline metrics designed to correlate with online product metrics** because online user testing is slow/expensive.
6. **PRELUDE/CIPHER** ([NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2404.15269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15269)): formalizes **user edit distance as the cost function** for an on-behalf writing agent, with an email-writing environment; preferences are inferred from edit history and success = declining cumulative edit cost.

**What's genuinely missing in the literature** (your opportunity): nobody scores the **retrieval/context-assembly stage** directly against sent replies — i.e., "extract the facts/threads the real reply depended on (via LLM decomposition of the sent reply), then measure whether the context assembler surfaced them." The closest analogues are LongMemEval's indexing/retrieval/reading decomposition and PerLTQA's classify/retrieve/synthesize split, both on synthetic data. Publishing-grade novelty aside, it's straightforwardly buildable and no published result contradicts its validity. One caution from the Smart Compose lineage: the sent reply is *a* good answer, not *the only* good answer — treat facts-the-reply-used as **recall** ground truth (context needed), never as precision ground truth (context that would have been wrong to include).

### 3.2 Synthetic query generation & RAGAS-class frameworks (2026 state)

- **Reliability critique**: RAGAS-metric correlation with human judgment measured at a harmonic mean of only ~0.55 in domain QA ([telecom RAG eval](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.12873)); "Can we Evaluate RAGs with Synthetic Data?" ([arXiv:2508.11758](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11758v1)) finds synthetic benchmarks are **decent for retriever parameter tuning but unreliable for ranking generators**. That's a usable rule: synthetic queries for tuning k/chunking/embedding choice; real held-out replies for anything that ranks generation pipelines.
- 2026 consensus pattern: **golden + synthetic + sampled-production, human-calibrated, strictly versioned** ([Label Your Data 2026 RAG eval guide](https://labelyourdata.com/articles/llm-fine-tuning/rag-evaluation), [Red Hat on synthetic RAG eval](https://developers.redhat.com/articles/2026/02/23/synthetic-data-rag-evaluation-why-your-rag-system-needs-better-testing), [multi-agent private synthetic RAG datasets](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18929)). Databricks' guide is a good practical reference for retrieval-quality golden sets ([docs](https://docs.databricks.com/gcp/en/vector-search/vector-search-retrieval-quality)).

## 4. End-task evals for on-behalf drafting

### 4.1 Counterfactual eval (hold out real sent replies)

The metric stack, from cheapest/weakest to strongest:
- **N-gram (BLEU/ROUGE)**: used by Panza; known to reward surface overlap and miss "different words, same voice, same outcome." Fine as a regression tripwire, not as truth.
- **Semantic similarity** to the real reply (embedding): catches content match, blind to style.
- **AV/style-embedding verifier**: "would an authorship verifier accept this as the user's writing, for *this recipient*?" — supported by the AV robustness result ([arXiv:2603.29454](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.29454)) and the style-eval paper's finding that embeddings beat LLM judges on style ([arXiv:2508.06374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06374)).
- **LLM judge with the real reply as reference**: graded rubric (content coverage / factual grounding / tone match / actionability), order-randomized, cross-family judge. Reference-based judging is much more reliable than reference-free.
- **Ensemble of the above** — the explicit recommendation of [arXiv:2508.06374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06374).

### 4.2 Live usage metrics (the only numbers that ultimately matter)

- **Acceptance rate** precedent: GitHub Copilot publishes ~27–33% suggestion acceptance ([GitHub research via ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/github-30-of-copilot-coding-suggestions-are-accepted), [Zoominfo deployment study: 33% suggestion / 20% line acceptance](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13282v1), [GitHub metrics docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/copilot-usage-metrics/copilot-metrics)). Documented caveat: **acceptance rate measures adoption, not quality** ([Software.com](https://docs.software.com/ai-impact/github-copilot-metrics), [Axify](https://axify.io/blog/github-copilot-metrics)) — pair it with post-acceptance survival (was the accepted draft then heavily edited before sending?).
- **Edit-distance-as-cost**: PRELUDE/CIPHER's cumulative-edit-cost formulation ([arXiv:2404.15269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15269)) is the right ledger for a personal agent: every send event yields (draft, final sent text) → normalized edit distance, bucketed as accepted-verbatim / lightly-edited / rewritten / discarded. No email product publishes these numbers (Superhuman/Shortwave/Gmail keep them internal); Copilot telemetry design is the best public template.
- **Human eval where the evaluator IS the user**: **PersonaMail** ([arXiv:2602.17340](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17340)) ran N=16 participants rating drafts for *their own* real communication scenarios (Likert quality, NASA-TLX load, QuickFix acceptance) — deliberately avoiding archived-message metrics; **Panza** authors evaluated on their own mailboxes. For a single-user system this collapses gracefully: N-of-1 blind A/B (show two drafts, pick one, log it) is methodologically fine as long as pipeline identity is hidden and order-randomized — the same discipline judge evals use.

## 5. Regression discipline for a continuously-changing private corpus

Patterns with 2026 sourcing:
- **Frozen snapshot corpora**: freeze a dated copy of the mailbox/message archive as the eval substrate; version golden sets alongside code; keep superseded slices as archived regression canaries re-run periodically — "if slice_v1_archived suddenly tanks, something fundamental shifted" ([Eval Set Drift](https://dev.to/gabrielanhaia/eval-set-drift-how-to-know-when-your-golden-set-went-stale-p8m), [golden dataset guide](https://qaskills.sh/blog/golden-dataset-llm-evaluation-guide), [RAG regression testing](https://qaskills.sh/blog/rag-regression-testing-guide)).
- **Judge drift control**: pin judge model version + temperature 0; changing judges requires re-baselining; maintain a **fixed anchor set with frozen human labels** to attribute drift to system-vs-judge — now formalized in "Who Drifted: the System or the Judge?" ([arXiv:2606.15474](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15474)); pin dated model snapshots, never floating aliases ([Braintrust eval guide](https://www.braintrust.dev/articles/llm-evaluation-guide)).
- **Cheap-vs-expensive tiering**: deterministic/n-gram/embedding checks on every commit; LLM-judge suites on merges/nightly; human (you) review weekly on a small stratified sample — the standard CI-gate pattern ([Galtea CI/CD quality gates](https://galtea.ai/blog/automated-llm-evaluation-building-a-ci-cd-quality-gate-that-actually-runs)).
- **Tooling for a solo self-hosted project (2026)**: **promptfoo** — open-source MIT CLI, declarative configs, CI-native, fully local; acquired by OpenAI in March 2026 but core committed to staying MIT/model-agnostic ([Braintrust's own comparison concedes this](https://www.braintrust.dev/articles/best-promptfoo-alternatives-2026)); Braintrust and LangSmith are closed-source with self-hosting enterprise-only ([comparison](https://www.braintrust.dev/articles/langsmith-alternatives-2026), [2026 framework benchmark](https://aiml.qa/llm-evaluation-framework-benchmark-2026/)). For a privacy-critical personal corpus, promptfoo (or DeepEval) + a local tracing store (e.g., Arize Phoenix, self-hostable) is the defensible stack; common 2026 practice is one dev-time framework + one observability layer ([Inference.net comparison](https://inference.net/content/llm-evaluation-tools-comparison/)).
- **Multi-seed + error bars**: the memory-benchmark fiasco's core lesson — ≥10 seeds, published σ, two judges from different families, treat <10pt deltas as noise ([Benchmark Theatre](https://essays.bloo-mind.ai/posts/2026-05-20-mem-eval/)).

## The eval plan I'd bet on for this project (concrete, staged)

**Stage 0 — Freeze the substrate (week 1).** Snapshot the full comms corpus at a dated cut (e.g., `corpus@2026-07-01`). Split **temporally**: everything before T is the system's world; sent messages after T are held-out targets. All Stage 1–3 evals run against this frozen snapshot; refresh quarterly with the old snapshot kept as a canary.

**Stage 1 — Baselines before architecture (weeks 1–2).** Before evaluating any memory/graph/profile architecture, score two dumb baselines on every eval below: (a) full-context/long-context stuffing, (b) filesystem + grep/BM25 agentic search (Letta-style). Any architecture that doesn't beat both by a clear margin on your own data is theater — this is the single strongest lesson from the memory-benchmark wars.

**Stage 2 — The reply-supervised context-assembly eval (the killer eval; weeks 2–4).** For each held-out sent reply: (1) an LLM decomposes the real reply into the atomic facts/threads/relationship knowledge it *used* (who this person is, prior commitments, tone register, open loops); (2) run the context assembler on the incoming message only; (3) score **fact-recall@k** — fraction of used-facts present in assembled context — plus token budget. This is recall-only supervision (the sent reply proves what was *needed*, not what was *forbidden*); measure precision separately via context-size budgets and a distractor-injection check. Build ~150–300 such cases stratified by relationship type and message type; hand-audit 30 decompositions yourself once (you are the only valid annotator). Prior art to cite in design: next-response-selection Recall@k, LaMP, Panza's data-playback golden responses.

**Stage 3 — Counterfactual drafting eval (weeks 3–6).** On the same held-out replies, generate full drafts and score with a fixed **ensemble**: ROUGE/embedding-sim (content), StyleDistance-class style embedding + AV-verifier accept-rate (voice, per-relationship), and a pinned cross-family reference-based LLM judge with order randomization (quality rubric). Calibrate the judge once against ~50 of your own blind pairwise choices; validate it rejects wrong-but-topical distractors. Report all runs with ≥5 seeds and σ.

**Stage 4 — Memory/profile probes (parallel).** Adapt LongMemEval's five-ability taxonomy (extraction, multi-session, temporal, updates, **abstention**) into ~100 hand-written probes over your real corpus ("what did X and I decide about Y in March," "what changed after Z's job move," plus unanswerable traps). Score with the same pinned judge + anchor set. This is your per-capability regression suite for the context engine itself.

**Stage 5 — Live ledger (from first real use, forever).** Log every (draft, final-sent) pair → normalized edit distance, bucketed accepted-verbatim / light-edit / rewrite / discard, sliced by relationship. This PRELUDE-style cumulative edit cost is the north-star metric; offline Stages 2–4 exist to predict it, and you should periodically check they actually correlate (the Smart Compose discipline).

**Harness:** promptfoo (or DeepEval) in CI, all local; tiered — deterministic + embedding metrics per commit, judge suites nightly, your own blind A/B ~20 pairs/week; judge model pinned, anchor set frozen, everything versioned with the corpus snapshot.

## Top 3 open questions

1. **Does the reply-decomposition step create circular supervision?** The same LLM family that decomposes replies into "needed facts" may bias fact-recall toward what LLMs find salient rather than what *you* needed. Needs a one-time human audit and ideally two decomposer families compared — no published methodology exists for this yet.
2. **What offline metric actually predicts your edit distance?** Nobody has published the correlation between counterfactual ensemble scores (Stage 3) and real acceptance/edit behavior (Stage 5) for a personal agent; if the correlation is weak, the offline suite must be re-weighted or the whole architecture-selection process rests on sand.
3. **How much per-relationship style data is enough for the AV verifier to be a valid judge?** AV models are benchmarked on authors with substantial text; your long-tail contacts may have 5 emails of history. Where the verifier's discrimination power collapses (few-shot relationships) you need a fallback metric — cluster relationships into register groups? — and that threshold is unmeasured in the literature.
