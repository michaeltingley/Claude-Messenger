# Personalized Generation, Style Mimicry & User/Relationship Modeling Without Fine-Tuning
**Deep research pass, current as of July 2026.** Maturity scale: **Mature** (replicated, deployed), **Solid** (peer-reviewed, some replication), **Emerging** (1–2 papers, promising), **Gap** (little/no direct work).

---

## 1. Personalized text generation with LLMs (no fine-tuning)

### Benchmarks: the LaMP family
- **LaMP** (Salemi et al.) — 7 tasks, classification + short-form generation, established that retrieval-augmented personalization (retrieving items from the user's own history into the prompt) beats non-personalized baselines. Canonical repo: https://github.com/LaMP-Benchmark/LaMP
- **LongLaMP** — long-form personalized generation, incl. **Personalized Email Completion** (explicitly motivated by "email tone varies significantly based on the recipient"): https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11016v1 / https://longlamp-benchmark.github.io/
- **LaMP-QA** (personalized long-form QA, 2025): https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00137v1
- **PersonaMem** (COLM 2025) — evolving user profiles across up to 60 multi-turn sessions; SOTA LLMs struggle with dynamic preference shifts: https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14225v1, https://github.com/bowen-upenn/PersonaMem. **PersonaMem-v2** (Dec 2025) adds *implicit* preferences + agentic memory: https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06688v1. This is the modern PersonaChat lineage — persona-as-user-model rather than persona-as-chitchat-card.
- Umbrella survey: **"A Survey of Personalized Large Language Models"** (2502.11528, v2 2025) — taxonomizes no-tuning methods into retrieval-augmented prompting, profile-augmented prompting, and soft-fused prompting (the last requires training, excluded for you): https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11528v2. Living list: https://github.com/VanillaCreamer/Awesome-Personalized-LLMs
**Maturity: Mature** (benchmarks), though all LaMP-family tasks model *the author only*, not the dyad.

### What actually moves the needle on style fidelity
- **Retrieval choice matters a lot.** Salemi et al., SIGIR 2024 ("Optimization Methods for Personalizing LLMs through Retrieval Augmentation") — training retrievers against downstream generation reward (ROPG-RL/KD) and dynamically *selecting* the retrieval strategy per input (RSPG) beats any fixed retriever; RSPG-Post best on 6/7 LaMP datasets: https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05970, https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3626772.3657783. Takeaway: which exemplars you pick is a first-order variable, worth optimizing.
- **Few-shot exemplars are the single biggest lever.** "How Well Do LLMs Imitate Human Writing Style?" (Sept 2025): few-shot prompting gives up to **23.5× higher style-matching accuracy than zero-shot**; completion-style prompting reaches ~99.9% style agreement — but outputs are less "unpredictable" than humans (perplexity 15.2 vs 29.5): https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24930
- **But ceiling is real for informal registers.** "Catch Me If You Can? Not Yet" (EMNLP 2025 Findings): 40k+ generations, 400+ real authors; ICL approximates style in *structured* domains (news, **email**) but fails on informal blog/forum voice; more demonstrations shows diminishing returns; measured by an ensemble of authorship attribution/verification + style matching + AI detection: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14543, https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.532.pdf. Good news for you: email/messaging is the domain where ICL works *best*; bad news: casual texting is closer to the hard informal end.
- **Exemplars vs distilled style guides: evidence says BOTH, exemplars first.** Google's "Teach LLMs to Personalize" (2308.07968) found the winning recipe is retrieval → ranking → **summarization** → synthesis → generation, i.e., retrieved examples *plus* a distilled summary, each contributing: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07968. STYLL (low-resource authorship style transfer, 2212.08986) prompts with LLM-generated *style descriptors* derived from target exemplars plus pseudo-parallel examples — descriptor+exemplar beats either alone: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.08986. CAT-LLM similarly combines explicit text-style definitions with examples: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.05707
- **RAG vs fine-tuning head-to-head**: "Comparing Retrieval-Augmentation and PEFT for Privacy-Preserving Personalization" (2409.09510) — RAG is competitive with PEFT on LaMP; combining is best, but RAG alone is a strong operating point: https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09510. **Panza** (2407.10994) shows fine-tuning+RAG on <100 emails works locally, and its ablations show RAG contributes materially even without training; its methodological gift is that **BLEU+MAUVE correlates strongly with human preference for personalized email**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10994
- Excluded by your constraint but notable: contrastive activation steering for personalization (StyleVector, 2503.05213) and Persona-Plug/USER-LLM user embeddings (2409.11901) require white-box access or trained encoders — not available via frontier APIs.
**Maturity: Solid.** Consensus recipe: retrieve the user's own most-relevant prior messages as exemplars, add a distilled style description, and prefer completion-style framing over "write in the style of" instructions.

## 2. Offline profile distillation & relationship modeling

### Distilling a corpus into a reusable artifact
- **PROSE** (2505.23815, "Aligning LLMs by Predicting Preferences from User Writing Samples"): iteratively infers a natural-language preference/style description from writing samples, *verifying* each inferred preference against held-out samples before keeping it. Beats CIPHER by 33%; **combined with ICL exemplars, +47% over CIPHER** — the strongest direct evidence that a validated distilled profile + exemplars stack: https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23815v1. Comes with the **PLUME** email/memo benchmark. **Maturity: Emerging-to-Solid; this is the paper closest to your exact pipeline.**
- **Persona-DB** (2402.11060): hierarchical refinement — raw History → Distilled Persona → Induced (generalized) Persona — improves response prediction and *reduces* required retrieval size: https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11060v1
- **Interpretable style representations**: LISA — "Learning Interpretable Style Embeddings via Prompting LLMs" (2305.12696) generates human-readable style attribute vectors: https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~ccb/publications/interpretable-style-embeddings.pdf; follow-up "Interpreting Style Representations via Style-Eliciting Prompts" (2606.05716) shows decoded style prompts can steer LLM outputs to match target style: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05716
- **Style embeddings for exemplar retrieval / verification**: **LUAR** (author representation) and **StyleDistance** (2410.12757, content-independent style embeddings via synthetic parallel data, SOTA on STEL): https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12757v1
- Survey of the persona/personalization split: "Two Tales of Persona in LLMs" (2406.01171): https://arxiv.org/html/2406.01171v1
**Maturity: Solid for individual profiles.** Structured, *evidence-validated* style rubrics (PROSE-style) beat naive "summarize their vibe" prompts.

### Relationship/dyad modeling — the striking gap
- **"Examining the Role of Relationship Alignment in LLMs"** (2410.01708, Facebook post-comment data): relationship attributes (closeness, gender, age) improve *prediction* of human comment tone, **but explicitly injecting relationship metadata into generation prompts *decreased* similarity to real human comments** — LLMs infer register better from the raw conversational text than from stated relationship labels: https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01708. This is the single most important negative result for your design: condition on the *dyad's actual message history*, not on "this is my mom."
- Audience/relationship tailoring works when grounded: recipient-personality-tailored persuasive messages significantly more influential (cited therein); persuasion style shifts with recipient attributes (2601.05751): https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.05751
- Dyadic interaction research exists mostly in affective computing (Meta Seamless Interaction; ACII DaiKon 2026 workshop https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.02672) and information-flow analysis (2606.30096), not in text-generation conditioning.
- **No published system represents a user↔contact dyad as a first-class distilled artifact ("relationship card": register, formality, in-jokes, typical length, emoji norms, topics) for generation.** LongLaMP's email task acknowledges recipient-dependence but doesn't model it.
**Maturity: Gap — this is your novelty opportunity and your main experimental risk.**

## 3. Automatic prompt optimization as the training loop
- **GEPA** (2507.19457, **ICLR 2026 oral**) is the current state of the art: reflective prompt evolution over natural-language execution traces + Pareto frontier of candidates. Beats MIPROv2 by >10pp aggregate, beats GRPO RL with ~35× fewer rollouts, works with as few as ~10 examples and 20–100 evals, produces prompts up to 9.2× shorter: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457, https://openreview.net/forum?id=RQm2KQTM5r, https://github.com/gepa-ai/gepa. Integrated into DSPy alongside **MIPROv2** (Bayesian search over instructions+demos; wants 200+ examples): https://www.morphllm.com/gepa-prompt-optimization
- **OPRO** (LLM-as-optimizer) and **TextGrad** (textual gradients; published in Nature, https://github.com/zou-group/textgrad) remain usable but are now generally outperformed by GEPA-class reflective evolution on compound pipelines; 2026 practice runs these inside eval harnesses as code: https://futureagi.com/blog/top-10-prompt-optimization-tools-2025/
- **Agentic Context Engineering (ACE)** (2510.04618) — evolving "playbook" contexts instead of weights; conceptually the bridge between prompt optimization and memory: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04618
- **Applicability to your case**: your setup is unusually well-suited — you have free supervision (held-out real sent replies) and a per-dyad metric (style-embedding similarity + LLM rubric judge). GEPA's per-instance natural-language feedback ("draft was too formal, user's real reply used lowercase + no sign-off") is exactly the signal shape it exploits. Caveat: nobody has published APO applied specifically to per-relationship style pipelines; leakage control (optimize the *shared* pipeline prompt on some dyads, validate on held-out dyads) is untested territory.
**Maturity: Mature tooling, Emerging application to style mimicry.**

## 4. Learning from implicit feedback (edits) without weight updates
- **PRELUDE/CIPHER** (2404.15269, NeurIPS 2024) — the canonical framework: infer latent preference descriptions from each edit, retrieve inferred preferences for similar future contexts; reduces subsequent edit cost, interpretable, no fine-tuning; evaluated on summarization and **email writing**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15269, https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/f75744612447126da06767daecce1a84-Paper-Conference.pdf
- **PROSE** (above) supersedes CIPHER on writing-sample-based preference inference (+33–47%): https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23815v1
- **Preference-Aware Memory Update for Long-Term LLM Agents** (2510.09720) — memory that adapts as preferences *evolve*: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09720v1. **Memento** (2508.16153) — "fine-tuning agents without fine-tuning LLMs" via case-based memory read/write: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16153
- The field is now also mining edits for *training* ("Principled Fine-tuning of LLMs from User-Edits," 2601.19055 — excluded for you, but its edit-taxonomy is reusable): https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.19055
- **Anthropic infrastructure**: the **memory tool** (beta since Sept 2025; file-based cross-session memory, CRUD by the agent) + context editing is the natural substrate for durable per-dyad preference files: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/memory-tool, https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/advanced-tool-use
**Maturity: Solid.** The pattern (edit → LLM-inferred preference sentence → retrieval-indexed memory, periodically consolidated PROSE-style with validation against held-out sends) is well-evidenced.

## 5. Style evaluation
- **Ensembles beat any single judge.** "Evaluating Style-Personalized Text Generation: Challenges and Directions" (2508.06374): BLEU-type, style embeddings, and LLM judges each correlate poorly with humans alone; ensembles consistently win; includes a style-discrimination benchmark across 8 tasks: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.06374
- **Authorship verification as automatic judge**: LUAR and **StyleDistance** embeddings are standard for measuring whether generated text matches a reference author (https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12757v1); benchmarked against LLM judges in "Attribution Quality in AI-Generated Content" (2510.13898): https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.13898. Notably, **AV methods are robust judges**: "Authorship Impersonation via LLM Prompting does not Evade Authorship Verification" (2603.29454) — prompted imitation still gets caught, so AV scores are a meaningful (hard-to-game) fitness function: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29454. Cross-genre AV benchmark: CROSSNEWS (AAAI 2025): https://cocoxu.github.io/publications/AAAI_2025_Cross_genre_Authorship.pdf
- **LLM-as-judge pitfalls**: self-preference bias — judges overrate outputs in their own generation style, stronger in bigger models (2410.21819: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819); plus verbosity/position bias (survey 2411.16594: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.16594). For *style* judging this is acute: a Claude judge scoring Claude drafts will systematically reward Claude-flavored smoothness over the user's actual messiness. Mitigate with rubric-anchored judging + embedding metrics + pairwise "which is the real reply?" discrimination tests.
- **Cheap human-correlated proxy**: BLEU+MAUVE combo validated against human preference for personalized email (Panza, 2407.10994).
- **Recipient-conditioned eval**: **no benchmark exists for "did this sound like the author writing to THIS recipient."** Closest: LongLaMP email completion (recipient-agnostic) and the Facebook relationship-alignment study. You'd have to build it — fortunately trivially, from held-out real (thread, sent-reply) pairs per dyad.
**Maturity: Solid for author-level; Gap for dyad-level.**

## 6. Sycophancy / drift / mode-collapse risks
- **Persona drift is real and fast**: self-consistency degrades >30% after 8–12 turns; identity drift in agent conversations (2412.00804: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.00804); overview: https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/persona-drift. Less critical for you since each draft is a fresh, short, context-assembled call — drift risk lives in the *long-lived memory artifacts*, not the conversation.
- **Personalization amplifies sycophancy**: "Personalization Methods Should Address Sycophancy" (https://personalization-sycophancy.github.io/assets/paper.pdf); interaction context increases sycophancy (CHI 2026: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3791915); social sycophancy taxonomy **ELEPHANT** (2505.13995: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.13995); causes/mitigations survey (2411.15287: https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15287v1). Concrete failure mode for you: an edit-feedback loop where the profile updater over-generalizes each accepted draft drifts the "voice" toward whatever the model already likes writing — path-dependent homogenization is documented in personalized-feedback risk taxonomies (2303.05453: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.05453).
- **Mode collapse in imitation**: even high-fidelity imitations are measurably *blander* than the human (perplexity 15.2 vs 29.5, 2509.24930), and "LLMs optimized to fool detectors still have a distinct style" (2505.14608: https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14608v1). Defenses: keep raw exemplars (not just distilled rubrics) in every generation prompt; validate profile updates against held-out real messages (PROSE's verification step); periodically re-anchor profiles to the raw corpus rather than to prior profile+drafts.
**Maturity: Solid awareness, Emerging mitigations.**

## What I'd bet on for this project

1. **Per-dyad retrieval-first generation**: for each draft, retrieve the user's real messages *to that contact* (recency + situation-similarity via embeddings, RSPG-style multi-strategy) as completion-framed exemplars. This is the best-evidenced single lever (23.5× few-shot effect; email is the domain where ICL works best).
2. **Two-layer offline distillation, validated PROSE-style**: (a) a global "author card" (lexicon, punctuation/casing habits, structural tics) and (b) a per-contact "relationship card" (register, length norms, emoji/slang, greetings/sign-offs, running topics/in-jokes, asymmetries). Keep every distilled claim only if it survives checking against held-out real messages in that dyad. Crucially — per the relationship-alignment negative result — derive register from the dyad's *actual transcript*, never from a stated relationship label.
3. **GEPA (via DSPy) as the offline training loop**: optimize the shared pipeline prompt against held-out real sent replies, scored by an ensemble judge = StyleDistance/LUAR similarity + rubric-anchored LLM judge (different model family than the generator) + a pairwise "pick the real reply" discriminator. It's sample-efficient enough (10s of examples per dyad) for a single user's data.
4. **Edit-delta memory as the online loop**: CIPHER/PROSE pattern on Anthropic's memory tool — every user edit becomes an inferred preference sentence filed under the dyad; periodic consolidation with validation, and periodic re-anchoring to raw corpus to fight sycophantic drift.

## Top 3 open questions needing experiments

1. **Does an explicit relationship card add anything over pure dyad-exemplar retrieval?** The Facebook result suggests explicit relationship metadata can *hurt*; PROSE suggests validated distilled profiles *help*. Which wins for dyad register — and does card+exemplars beat exemplars alone — is unresolved and directly testable on your own held-out sent messages per contact.
2. **How much per-dyad data is enough, and what's the fallback curve?** Below what message count does a dyad-specific pipeline underperform a global-style + inferred-register baseline (for new/sparse contacts)? Nobody has published this curve for informal messaging.
3. **Can the eval ensemble detect the failures that matter?** Specifically: does a StyleDistance/LUAR + cross-family LLM-judge ensemble reliably distinguish your real replies from Claude's best imitation *per dyad* (if judges can't tell them apart, optimization is blind), and does it catch sycophantic blandness drift over weeks of edit-feedback updates? Requires a small longitudinal self-experiment before trusting the loop to auto-send.
